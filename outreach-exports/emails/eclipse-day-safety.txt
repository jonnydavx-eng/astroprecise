SUBJECT: Tonight: nine-tenths of the Sun, then the Perseids
It's today.

When to look (UK): the Moon first touches the Sun's disc between 18:08 and 18:17 BST, depending where you are. The eclipse deepens through the hour, reaching maximum at about 19:05 BST in Edinburgh, 19:12 in London and 19:16 in Truro — between 88% and 96% of the Sun covered, deepest in the far south-west. It is all over by around 20:10 BST, comfortably before sunset: the Sun does not set eclipsed anywhere in Britain. Greatest eclipse worldwide is at 18:46 BST (17:46 UT), 45 km off western Iceland — a different moment from our maximum, and not a UK time. The totality track crosses Greenland, Iceland and northern Spain; Britain never reaches totality. From southern Britain this is the deepest eclipse since 1999, though March 2015 was deeper in Scotland.

The one safety rule: never look at the Sun without ISO 12312-2 eclipse glasses — and because Britain never reaches totality, they stay on the whole way through. There is no safe moment tonight to take them off. Even at 96% covered the remaining crescent is bright enough to damage your eyes permanently. Use ISO 12312-2 glasses (the standard is printed on them), or project the image through a pinhole onto paper or the ground, or simply watch the light change around you. Ordinary sunglasses are not protection — not ever, not a little.

Afterwards: stay out. The Perseid meteor shower peaks tonight, and a moonless-enough late evening gives you a real chance of a fine show. An eclipse followed by meteors is the sort of double bill the sky rarely schedules.
