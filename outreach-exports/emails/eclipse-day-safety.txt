SUBJECT: Tonight: 91% of the Sun, then the Perseids
It's today.

When to look (UK): the Moon first touches the Sun's disc in the early evening, shortly after 18:00 BST in the south. The eclipse deepens through the hour, reaching maximum — about 91% of the Sun covered in the south of England — around 19:05 BST. The Sun sets this evening still partly eclipsed. Greatest eclipse globally is at 17:46 UT, over the Arctic; the totality track crosses Greenland, Iceland and northern Spain. From Britain, this is the deepest eclipse since 1999.

The one safety rule: never look at the Sun without proper eclipse glasses. At 91% covered, the remaining crescent is still bright enough to damage your eyes permanently. Use certified eclipse glasses, or project the image through a pinhole onto paper or the ground. Ordinary sunglasses are not protection — not ever, not a little.

Afterwards: stay out. The Perseid meteor shower peaks tonight, and a moonless-enough late evening gives you a real chance of a fine show. An eclipse followed by meteors is the sort of double bill the sky rarely schedules.
