Instrument: Ralph / Multispectral Visible Imaging Camera (MVIC), three colour filters,
New Horizons Pluto flyby 2015-07-14. Mosaic assembled by JHUAPL / SwRI.

License: NASA imagery — public domain. Credit required as
"NASA / Johns Hopkins APL / SwRI".

Projection: equirectangular, native 5926x2963 (exactly 2:1). Longitude runs 0-360 with
0 deg at the left edge, so IAU longitude 180 lands at map centre — Sputnik Planitia (the
left lobe of the "heart") sits at the centre, which matches the NASA caption and the USGS
mosaic convention. Verified from the pixels, not assumed.

Processing: ffmpeg lanczos resize only. No painting, no colour grading, no invented terrain.

Coverage note (measured, not guessed):
Everything south of about -55 deg latitude is black, and rows are only fully populated from
+90 down to about -29 deg. 30% of the map area carries no data. That is real: Pluto's
southern hemisphere was in polar night during the flyby and was never imaged. The missing
hemisphere was NOT painted in. A globe using this map will show an unlit southern cap —
that is Pluto as New Horizons actually saw it.

Thin horizontal hairline below/left of the heart (checked 2026-08-14 — official, not ours):
There is a faint bright hairline crossing the dark Cthulhu Macula terrain south-west of
Sputnik Planitia. It is in the NASA source. Cropping the same region from the 5926x2963
original and from the shipped 2048x1024 file gives a mean absolute difference of 1.72/255
(max 24) — JPEG requantisation only, with no structure added. It is a mosaic seam in the
official product, where lower-resolution approach imagery meets the high-resolution encounter
imagery. Leave it: removing it would mean retouching NASA's mosaic.

Wrap convention (correct, unlike the OPAL maps):
This map is an exclusive 0-360 grid — column 0 and the last column differ about as much as any
ordinary neighbouring pair (3.83 vs 2.36 at full resolution), so there is no duplicated
meridian and the texture tiles correctly. The wrap gap in the shipped 2048 file sits at the
36th percentile of all column-to-column steps, i.e. the seam is unremarkable. A periodic
resample was tried and moved it to the 46th percentile — no visible gain, so not shipped.

Why the colour mosaic and not the higher-resolution USGS product:
USGS Astrogeology also publishes a 300 m/px global mosaic
(https://planetarymaps.usgs.gov/mosaic/Pluto_NewHorizons_Global_Mosaic_300m_Jul2017_8bit.tif,
296 MB, public domain, LORRI + MVIC). It is far sharper but single-band panchromatic —
grayscale only. Using it would mean either a grey Pluto or inventing a colour tint, so the
real MVIC colour mosaic was chosen instead. The USGS grayscale mosaic and its matching DEM
remain the right source if a height/detail pass is ever wanted.
