Processing: ImageMagick resize only to 2048x1024 (full webp+jpg), 1024x512 (_md webp), 512x256 (_sm webp). No painting, no invented bands.

Native resolution:
Both official OPAL global maps are only 721x361 (0.5 deg/px). The shipped 2048x1024 tier is
therefore a ~2.8x upscale of the source — the file is 2K but the detail is not. Verified
against the archive TIFFs at 721x361: correlation 0.9999, mean abs difference 0.35/255, so
the shipped pixels are faithful to the official product. The soft rainbow striping along the
coverage edge is per-filter misregistration in the STScI product, magnified by that upscale.

Coverage note (measured from the archive TIFFs, not guessed):
Uranus 2025a carries data from +90 down to about -19.5 deg latitude only, and rows are fully
populated only to about -9 deg. 40% of the map area is black. Uranus's southern hemisphere is
turned away from Earth and in polar night, so Hubble cannot see it. That is Hubble's real map,
not a convert error. The missing hemisphere was NOT painted in.
Neptune 2025b is NOT full-latitude (an earlier version of this note claimed it was). It runs
from about +71 deg down to the south pole, fully populated only below about +58 deg, leaving
13% of the map black at the north.

On a globe (rendered check, three.js sphere, 2026-08-14):
Neptune reads fine — one small misregistration patch at the north pole. Uranus does not: the
unobserved 40% becomes a black southern cap with a hard rainbow band across the middle of the
disc, which reads as a rendering fault rather than a planet. The pixels are the official
product, so this is a "which product" decision and not a conversion bug. Left as shipped
rather than re-authored, because painting the missing hemisphere would be invention and
swapping the source is the helm's call. The previous Solar System Scope Uranus map is still in
git history at 5ca7352 if the smooth full-globe map is preferred for Act 1.

Colour note:
These are narrow/medium-band filter composites, not true-colour photographs. Uranus lands
close to its familiar pale cyan. Neptune comes out green-dominant (lit-pixel mean RGB
100/192/156) rather than the iconic deep blue, because F547M dominates the composite. That
green cast is in the official STScI product and has been left alone — it is also closer to
the truth than the poster image: Irwin et al. (2024) reprocessed Voyager 2 data and found
Neptune is a much paler greenish-blue, the deep blue being a contrast-stretch artefact.
Pushing this map toward Voyager blue would be invention, so it was not done.
