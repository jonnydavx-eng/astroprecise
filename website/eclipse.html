Eclipse times across the UK

The partial eclipse runs from roughly 18:00 to 20:10 BST, with the deepest moment between about 18:55 and 19:15 BST depending on where you stand. Typical figures:

Look safely. Even at 91% coverage the remaining sliver of Sun is dangerously bright — never look directly. Use certified eclipse glasses (ISO 12312-2), a pinhole projector, or simply watch the world change around you: the strange light, the sharpened shadows, the sudden cool.

Where is it total?

Totality — the full black-Sun spectacle — crosses the Arctic, western Iceland and northern Spain. It's Europe's first total solar eclipse since 2006. Over Britain the eclipse is deep partial: no darkness, but an unmistakable dimming unlike anything since August 1999.

The same night: the Perseids

By rare coincidence, the Perseid meteor shower — the most-watched of the year — peaks overnight on 12–13 August 2026 under dark, moonless skies, with up to around 100 meteors an hour after midnight. Stay out after the eclipse: the sky's second act is free.
