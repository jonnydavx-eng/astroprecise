Wednesday 12 August is the deepest solar eclipse over Britain since August 1999. It is not a total eclipse here, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something. The Moon's dark shadow crosses Greenland, Iceland and northern Spain. Britain sits outside that band and gets a deep partial instead — 88 to 96% of the Sun covered depending where you stand, deepest in the far south-west.

Times, all BST, all low in the west:
first contact 18:08–18:17 · maximum 19:05 Edinburgh, 19:12 London, 19:16 Truro · last contact ≈20:10, before sunset everywhere

Greatest eclipse globally is 18:46 here, 17:46 UT. That moment belongs to a patch of sea 45 km off western Iceland, not to us.

One thing almost nobody mentions. A solar eclipse can only happen at new moon — so the Moon is out of the way all night, and the Perseids peak into a properly dark sky the same night. Eclipse first, meteors after.
