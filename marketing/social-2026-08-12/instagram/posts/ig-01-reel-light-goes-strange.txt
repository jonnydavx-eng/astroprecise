Four days.

Wednesday evening the Moon moves in front of the Sun and stays there for about two hours. From Britain it never quite covers it — about 91% at maximum in the south, a little less further north. Totality runs through Iceland and northern Spain. Not here.

So it won't go dark. It goes strange, which is better: the light drains out of an ordinary August evening while the sky still looks like day. Shadows sharpen. Colours flatten. It gets cold. The birds stop.

Maximum is around 19:05 BST, low in the west. Pick your horizon now.

The free page computes where the eclipse point — 20°02′ Leo — falls in your own chart. It runs on your device. Nothing uploaded, nothing stored. If it touches nothing in your chart, the page says so.

The weather report of the sky, not prophecy.

Link in bio.

Never look at the Sun without ISO 12312-2 eclipse glasses. Pinhole projection works. Sunglasses are not protection.
