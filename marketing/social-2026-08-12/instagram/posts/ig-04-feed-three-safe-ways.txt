Three safe ways to watch on Wednesday. Squinting is not one of them.

1 — ISO 12312-2 eclipse glasses. Check the standard is actually printed on them. Buy today, not on Wednesday afternoon. We sell none of these and take no cut on them; we would simply like you to keep your sight.

2 — Projection. Binoculars or a small telescope aimed at a white card, as in the picture, and you look at the card. The free version works just as well: a pinhole in one sheet of card, held above a second sheet.

3 — Watch the light instead. Underrated, and the only method that costs nothing and risks nothing. Around 19:05 the whole landscape changes character. Look at how sharp the shadows go. Look under a tree — every gap between the leaves is its own pinhole camera, and the ground fills up with little crescents.

Once, plainly: never look at the Sun directly. At 91% the remaining sliver is still bright enough to burn your retina permanently, and Britain never reaches totality, so there is no safe moment at all on Wednesday. Sunglasses, phone cameras, smoked glass and exposed film are not protection.
