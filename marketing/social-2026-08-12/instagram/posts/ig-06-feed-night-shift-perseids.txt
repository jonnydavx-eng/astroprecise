The Perseids peak tonight, and tonight is a new moon, because a new moon is the only kind of night a solar eclipse can happen on. No moonlight, no washout. The best Perseid conditions in years, on the same date, by geometry rather than luck.

How to actually see them: get away from a security light, give your eyes twenty minutes to adapt, and then do not look at your phone, because that resets them. Lie back so you take in as much sky as possible. Don't stare straight at the radiant in Perseus, low in the north-east — the long ones show up off to the side. Rates climb after midnight.
