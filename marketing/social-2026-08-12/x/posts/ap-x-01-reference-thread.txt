═══ 1/5 ═══

Britain does not see totality on Wednesday. The Moon's shadow crosses Iceland and northern Spain — not us.

What Britain gets is a deep partial, the deepest since 1999, and it is stranger than that sounds.

The real numbers, and what to actually expect:

═══ 2/5 ═══

Computed, not copied:

Across the UK, roughly 89–92% of the Sun goes. The spread from Cornwall to Shetland is small — almost everyone gets a deep one.

First bite shortly after 18:00 BST.
Maximum around 19:05 BST.
All of it low in the west.

═══ 3/5 ═══

The mistake everyone makes: 91% covered is not 91% dark.

Your eye rebalances nearly the whole way. It won't look like dusk. It looks like the wrong kind of afternoon — flat, metallic, colour drained, shadows gone strange.

Watch the land. That's where it shows.

═══ 4/5 ═══

Two times get muddled every eclipse.

Greatest eclipse — the deepest point anywhere on Earth — is 17:46 UT. That's 18:46 BST, and it happens over the Arctic.

Britain's own maximum is later: about 19:05 BST.

Different moment, different place. Both real.

═══ 5/5 ═══

Practical bit: find a clear western horizon today, not at 19:00 on Wednesday.
