## What I looked at first

I read the video rather than the filename, via `creative-reset/frames/sheet-original-12s.png` and
the full-res last frame.

Three things are true and both fatal:

- **The crescent is a Moon.** Dark night-side, warm limb, deep-blue post-sunset sky over unlit
  fields. A 91% partial Sun is a searing sliver in still-bright daylight. Confirms the study finding.
- **The camera abandons the subject.** The armillary is in frame to ~2.5s, cranes out to ~5.5s, and
  is gone by frame 14. Frames 18-23 are visually identical — six seconds of no new information.
- **The register is wrong for the brief.** Brass, dry-stone, English dusk, documentary gold. It is a
  beautiful advert for the Royal Institution. It is aimed at the 10% who already respect astronomy.

Everything below is built to make those three mistakes structurally impossible, not just avoided.

## The idea

Point the camera **at the ground**.

On Wednesday afternoon, the gaps between leaves act as pinhole cameras. Every dapple of sunlight on
the pavement stops being a round blob and becomes a **crescent** — hundreds of them, all facing the
same way, scattered over tarmac and weeds and a kerb.

That is the whole film. Ordinary grey British ground, going quietly strange.

**Why the ground and not the sky:**

1. **It makes the argument visually.** A once-in-a-generation cosmic alignment, and where does it
   actually land? On a driveway. That *is* "it lands on nothing," said without a word.
2. **It cannot repeat the fatal error.** There is no sun in frame, no sky, no horizon. You cannot
   mis-render a Sun as a Moon if you never show either one. The previous generation's worst failure
   becomes physically unavailable to the model.
3. **It is the safe way to watch, demonstrated.** Leaf-gap projection is pinhole projection. The ad
   teaches the safe method by being made of it — no instruction needed, no one looking up.
4. **It is anti-slop by construction.** Tarmac, weeds and dead leaves. No purple gradient, no brass,
   no synthetic humans, no hands, no faces. In the year "slop" won word of the year, for a brand
   whose whole pitch is honesty, this is the only defensible thing to generate.
5. **Anyone can film it.** Which is the point — see *Do this for real* at the bottom.

**The colour is the other half.** Not gold nostalgia, not mystical purple. The true look of a deep
partial is a flat, cool, **silvery** daylight — like an underexposed photo of a bright day — with
shadow edges that go unnaturally sharp. It is a genuinely distinctive register and it belongs to no
one else. It is also simply what Wednesday will look like.

## Why it lands with THIS audience

Citing the study phase; I have not contradicted it anywhere.

- **It speaks the fluent vocabulary and none of the technical tier.** On screen: *eclipse, Leo,
  chart, placements*. Banned and absent: sextile, orb, midheaven, ephemeris, arcminute. Search
  demand sits exactly where the copy sits (birth chart 673k/mo, moon sign 74k, rising 74k); aspects
  and orb are a tier above this audience.
- **The anti-woo points at the industry, never the viewer.** The joke is the *feed* — "everyone will
  call it a portal" — and "everyone" includes us. Pew's most engaged segments (women under 50, LGBTQ
  adults at 54% yearly) are never the butt of it. They are the people being handed permission.
- **Anti-flattery is proven in this exact category.** Co-Star won on blunt black-and-white tone
  explicitly set against "soft pastels and mystical imagery." We are doing the tone, not the
  nihilism — because 2026 reviews flag that pure limitation leaves users "deflated rather than
  illuminated." Hence the fifth beat: *quiet charts are real*. Bluntness has a floor and this is it.
- **Belief here is held lightly.** Pew: 20% engage "just for fun" vs 10% who take insight from it;
  only 1% lean on it heavily. A joke about the discourse is safe. A joke about believers is not.
- **It is Reali-TEA, straight off TikTok Next 2026.** Polished fantasy giving way to shared truth
  and humour; polished ads losing to feed-native content. A driveway is feed-native. An armillary
  sphere on a dry-stone wall is a commercial.
- **It answers April 2024's countercurrent instead of ignoring it.** The UK is structurally the
  outside-the-path "that was it?" case. Rather than overselling darkness we get ahead of the
  underwhelm and make the underwhelm the content.
- **The Co-Star/Midjourney moment is live and we are its opposite.** Two weeks ago 4.3M actives
  learned their birth data now sits inside an AI image company. Our fifth-second claim is *computed
  on your phone, nothing uploaded*. That is not a feature line this month, it is a wedge.
- **It fixes the measured retention collapse.** The old cut's visual-cortex proxy fell 40% from t=2
  to t=11 while mind-wandering rose — because the camera left its subject and the last six seconds
  were a still. Here the frame never leaves the ground and the crescents thin, dim and shimmer
  continuously for the full runtime. Every second carries new information.

## The honest core — what the ad may and may not claim

Checked against the live `eclipse.html`, because accuracy is the brand.

**True and usable:**
- Free, computed in-browser, nothing uploaded, no account. (Page states all four.)
- It tells you when the eclipse misses your chart, in words, before recommending anything.
- Eclipse point 20°02' Leo. On screen this becomes "in Leo" — never the degree notation.
- UK sees a deep partial only. Totality is Iceland and northern Spain.

**Must not appear:**
- **Any price, any buy button, any "before you pay."** Checkout is closed — the page itself says
  *NO FAKE CHECKOUT* and only offers a notify list. The page's own "we tell you before you pay" line
  cannot be reused in the ad, because in the ad there is nothing to pay. The free compute is the
  entire offer.
- Prophecy, "portal", "manifest", or any claim the eclipse does something to you.
- Anyone looking at the sun, any sun in frame, any suggestion of removing glasses.

**Estimate flagged, not asserted:** with a 5° threshold and ~12 chart points, roughly **7 in 10
charts should come back quiet** (1 − (1 − 10/360)^12 ≈ 29% get a contact). That is arithmetic, not a
measurement of the shipped code. It supports the on-screen wording "mostly, nothing" — do **not** put
the number itself on screen until someone runs it against the real engine.

**Fact conflict to resolve before this ships.** The live page says "PEAK ≈19:05 BST" for Britain. The
study phase has London 19:12, Edinburgh 19:05, Truro 19:16. 19:05 looks like Edinburgh generalised to
the whole country. The video therefore says **"just after 7pm"** and nothing tighter. Someone should
decide which is right and fix the page, because the page is the one making the specific claim.

## Shot list — one shot, 11 seconds, no cuts

The camera never leaves the subject. That is a hard rule this time.

| Time | What is on screen |
|---|---|
| 0.0–1.0 | Locked frame, pointed straight down at grey tarmac: kerb edge, weeds in the cracks, a few dry leaves. The dapples of light are **already unmistakably crescent-shaped**, not round. Frame one is the hook — the light is visibly wrong and you don't yet know why. |
| 1.0–3.5 | A breeze moves the unseen leaves overhead. The crescents shimmer, slide, break apart and re-form. All of them face the same direction. |
| 3.5–6.5 | The crescents thin. Ambient light drops and drains of warmth toward flat silver; the shadow edges get sharper, not softer. |
| 6.5–9.0 | Thinnest point. Dozens of hairline crescents scattered across ordinary ground. Very slow 5% push-in — the only camera move in the film. |
| 9.0–11.0 | They begin, barely, to thicken again. The light lifts a fraction. Hold and end on the ground. |

No tilt up. No reveal. No sky. No sun. The film ends where it started, which is the joke.

## Generation prompt — seedance_2_0, text-to-video

Vertical 9:16, 11s, photoreal, no text baked in. **Text-to-video deliberately** — i2v with an
uploaded reference was jamming in moderation ("IP check not finished") and this concept needs no
reference, so the jam is designed around rather than fought.

```
Photoreal vertical 9:16 locked-off shot looking straight down at ordinary grey British tarmac:
the edge of a suburban driveway, a low concrete kerb, weeds growing in the cracks, a scatter of
dry leaves and grit. Dappled sunlight falls onto the ground through unseen tree leaves overhead.
From the very first frame every patch of light is clearly crescent-shaped rather than round —
dozens of small identical crescents scattered across the tarmac, all curving the same direction.
Across the shot the crescents slowly thin to hairlines and then barely begin to thicken again,
while the ambient daylight dims and drains of warmth into a flat, cool, silvery light like a
slightly underexposed photograph of a bright day, and the shadow edges become unnaturally sharp.
A light breeze stirs the unseen leaves so the crescents shimmer, slide, break and re-form
continuously. The camera stays fixed on the ground for the entire shot with one very slow 5%
push-in; it never tilts, never pans away, never reveals the sky.
Natural documentary photography, real 35mm lens, medium depth of field, fine asphalt and gravel
texture, realistic contact shadows, subtle handheld micro-movement only.
Negative: no sky, no horizon, no sun, no moon, no stars, no night, no people, no hands, no feet,
no faces, no animals, no vehicles, no text, no captions, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no
watermarks, no lens flare, no bokeh orbs, no sparkles, no glowing particles, no purple or violet
grade, no orange teal grade, no golden hour warmth, no time-lapse streaking, no camera crane,
no zoom out, no reveal shot.
```

Cost note: ~78 credits for 12s of seedance_2_0 against ~579 available. One generation, judged on a
contact sheet before any second attempt. Request 1080x1920 minimum — the last promo was 720p and the
Reel was an upscale of it, which adds pixels and not detail.

**Two checks on the returned file, before anything else:** the crescents must all curve the same way
(a real projection does; a hallucinated pattern won't), and the sky must never enter frame.

## Do this for real

The strongest version of this ad is not generated at all.

It is a phone on a driveway under a tree at about ten past seven on Wednesday, filmed by whoever is
holding it. Same shot, same beats, same caption, zero credits, and completely immune to the one
criticism this brand cannot survive — shipping AI footage while selling honesty.

My recommendation: **generate this as the pre-eclipse teaser** (it has to run before Wednesday, so
there is no real footage to use yet), then **re-shoot the identical frame on the day** and run the
real one as the post. The two-post arc writes itself: *here's what it'll look like* → *here's what it
actually looked like*. The second one is the honest one, and it costs nothing.

If only one ships, the generated teaser is defensible: abstract light on ordinary ground, no
synthetic people, nothing pretending to be a document of an event that hasn't happened yet.
