The instrument

What it computes, and what it needs from you

Positions
Ten placements — the Sun, the Moon, and Mercury through Pluto — as geocentric ecliptic longitude, placed to the arcminute. Aspects and orbs are computed from those positions, not rounded to a sign.
Angles and houses
Ascendant, Midheaven and house cusps, derived from local sidereal time at your exact latitude and longitude. Equal, Placidus or whole sign.
Accuracy
Typically within a few arcminutes for dates between 1800 and 2200 CE. The date field will not go outside that range: better no chart than a quietly wrong one.
Where it runs
In this browser, on your device. The only thing that leaves it is the place name you type, sent to look up its coordinates. Your chart is never uploaded.

Why the form is on the next page

A chart needs a place, not just a date. Without coordinates there is no Rising sign, no Midheaven and no houses — only the signs the planets sat in.

So there is no cut-down form here pretending otherwise. The real one lives on the chart page, where it can ask for your birth city, find its coordinates, and give you the whole chart. It takes about twenty seconds.

A weather report of the sky, not prophecy.

We compute where things were and where they are going. We do not claim to know what will happen to you. The method is published and the numbers are checkable.

That cuts both ways. When the August eclipse makes no close contact with your chart, the eclipse page says so plainly — before it quotes a price, and it recommends you skip the £2.99 reading. A quiet chart is told it is quiet.

The chart itself is free, and stays free. Written readings run £2.99 to £14 if you ever want one: a single payment, no subscription, nothing to cancel.
